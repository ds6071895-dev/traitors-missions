Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: one production texture atlas for a polished stylised underwater salvage videogame set in a Scottish highland sea loch. Create a square 2048x2048 texture sheet, exactly a clean 4 by 4 grid of equal square material tiles, edge to edge, no gutters, no labels or text, orthographic flat albedo surface scans with no perspective. Every tile fills its square completely. Hand-painted refined low-poly game aesthetic, crisp natural surface detail, softly illustrated, appealing realistic materials without photoreal noise. Row 1 left to right: pale coral-cream seabed sand in fine parallel ripples with tiny shell fragments; coarse shell and maerl gravel in broken pink, white and grey pieces; dark olive-grey settled silt with faint worm casts and a soft powdery surface; cold grey water-worn bedrock with mineral strata and rounded edges. Row 2: encrusting reef coral in patches of warm orange, violet and lime growing over each other; flat pink coralline algae crust on rock with fine white nodules; dense olive-brown kelp fronds lying in one direction with long central ribs; white and honey barnacle and blue mussel encrustation packed tight. Row 3: rusted riveted steel ship plate, orange-brown oxide over grey, regular rivet lines; peeling ivory and faded teal painted hull with flaking edges and thin weed streaks; tarred weathered deck planking running vertically with open dark seams; heavily corroded dark iron with scattered chain links and pitted wear. Row 4: wet grey highland beach shingle, rounded flat pebbles in mixed greys and browns; pale machair turf seen from directly overhead, fine close grass with tiny wildflowers; dense purple heather mixed with ochre bracken seen from above; snow-flecked pale highland granite scree in angular broken pieces. Critical: paint every tile as if lit by neutral white daylight. No blue or green underwater colour cast, no water caustics, no light shafts, no wetness highlights — the game adds all of that at runtime. Keep every tile in a restrained mid value range, average lightness near fifty percent, no deep black shadows and no blown white highlights, because the tiles are multiplied over the game's own colours. Restrained soft ambient shading only, no cast shadows, no bevelled grid borders, no objects, no text, no empty margins. Tiles should be internally seamless-looking and their detail legible on 3D geometry from thirty metres away.

Save the result as assets/dive/loch-materials.png. Tile order (row-major) is read by js/dive/materials.js:
  sand, maerl, silt, bedrock / coral, coralline, kelp, barnacle / steel, hullPaint, timber, iron / shingle, machair, heather, granite
Any square size works (every atlas in this repo has come back 1254x1254); cells are cropped 2px in from each edge and normalised by their own mean brightness, so a darker or lighter generation changes grain, not the loch's colours. Until the file exists the loch renders exactly as it does without it.
